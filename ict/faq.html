Asking and updates

1. I have a question. Where do I ask?

Bring doubts about the content to the live sessions, where everyone benefits from the answer. For anything else, email as described under Questions on the course page, with HB302 and your topic in the subject line.

2. Where will I get notices and updates?

On this website only, on the Announcements page. Check it regularly. Zoom links are the one exception: they are not posted here because this website is public, so you get them in the teaching team's emails or in the course WhatsApp group.

3. I am new to computers or to GitHub. Where do I start?

Start with Level 0. It lists the accounts and devices you need, in order. Videos on GitHub are part of the ViBe course material, and there is a session where we set up GitHub accounts together.

Classes

4. When are the live classes?

Every Thursday and Friday, 11:00 to 11:50 AM (IST), online on Zoom. The first 10 minutes are for anything important; the rest is work time on GitHub and your projects. The course counts 4 hours a week: about 2 in these sessions and about 2 on your own for ViBe and the case studies. See the session plan.

5. I am unwell and cannot attend a live session. Whom do I tell?

Email the teaching team, before the session if you can. You may miss at most 2 live sessions over the whole course, so keep those for days you are unwell or something unavoidable comes up. Whether a medical certificate is needed for a longer absence will be added here.

6. I have watched the ViBe videos. Do I still need to attend the live sessions?

Yes. Live polls during the sessions count for 15% of your marks, and missing more than 2 sessions means you do not complete the course.

7. I find the course too fast. How do I cope?

With a mixed class we pace the sessions for the average student, so the course may feel fast at times. Watch the ViBe lessons again, ask in the live session, or write to us early. Do not wait until a deadline.

8. I find the course too slow.

Use the capstone to go further. Your Tenali contribution and your own project have no upper limit, so build something more ambitious.

9. The whole class wants to move a session for a genuine reason. Is that possible?

Ask at least one week in advance, through the chair of the class committee. The request should name a replacement slot that suits the teaching team and every student. Requests made at short notice will not be accepted.

Work and submission

10. How do I submit my work?

On GitHub only. All weekly work and your capstone go to GitHub; we do not take submissions by email or WhatsApp. The exact steps (which repository, file names and folders) will be added here.

11. Where are the case studies and peer reviews?

On a separate login-based page at samagama.in/hb302, which opens soon. You write your response there and review three classmates' responses. What you do and how the marks work are in the Handbook under Case studies. The opening date will be announced on the Announcements page.

12. Can I work with others?

For the Tenali contribution, yes: you work in pairs or small groups. Your own capstone project is built on your own. Case responses and peer reviews are always individual.

13. Can I use AI?

Not for the case studies: write your responses and your peer reviews yourself. For the capstone, AI coding tools are welcome; say which tools you used and how. See Using AI.

Marks and rules

14. Can I get an extension?

No. The course is four weeks long and your classmates are waiting to review your case responses. A late case response can still be submitted until that case's review deadline, and missed peer reviews can be appealed once per case. The details are under Deadlines.

15. How is my grade worked out?

From your total marks out of 100: ViBe quizzes 15%, live-session quizzes 15%, case studies 20% and the capstone 50%. The marks for each grade are in the grade table.

16. What counts as malpractice?

Submitting someone else's work as yours, copying a classmate's case response or peer review, or letting others copy yours. See Academic integrity.

Spurti points

17. What are Spurti points (SP)?

SP count the effort you put into the course, not how well it was marked. You earn the same points for writing a case response whether it scores high or low. They are shown on your Spurti page on the case-study site, next to your tentative marks.

18. How do I earn SP?

The course is worth 1,000 SP in all, shared out much as the marks are: the ViBe course 150, the live sessions 150, the case studies 288 and the capstone 410. In practice that is 15 SP for every 10% of the ViBe course you finish, 19 SP for each live session you join, 9 SP for each case response you submit, 9 SP for each peer review you submit, and 410 SP for finishing your capstone. More sessions and more cases mean more points become available as the course runs, so read your score as "x of the points available so far", which is how the page shows it.

Late work earns half. A case response submitted after its deadline is worth 5 SP instead of 9, and so is a peer review written after the review deadline — including where an appeal was approved and the work still counts for marks. The line in your SP bank says so: "submitted late — half points, 5 of 9". Remember that lateness already costs you in the marks as well; see question 14.

19. Are SP my marks?

No. Your grade comes from the marks in question 15 and nothing else. SP are a separate record of effort, kept so that work you put in outside the marked pieces still counts for something.

20. What do SP get me?

If you finish the course with 90% or more of the Spurti points available to you, you are eligible for grace marks. Eligible means considered, not awarded: how many marks, and to whom, is the teaching team's decision.

21. Can SP be taken away?

Yes. Breaking a course rule can cost you points. Every deduction appears in your SP bank as its own line, with the reason written out and the date it was made, so you always know what it was for and when. Nothing in your bank is ever quietly edited or deleted: if a deduction was made in error, the points are given back as a new line and both lines stay on record. Your points cannot go below zero.

22. What does breaking a rule cost?

If you do something this FAQ already answers, the first time costs 10% of the 1,000 points the whole course is worth — 100 SP, the second time 20% (200 SP), the third time 50% (500 SP). The amount is set by the rule, not by whoever records it, and it is the same share whether it happens in week one or week four. If you hold fewer points than the deduction, your bank goes to zero; it never goes below.

There is no fourth step. After three, and for anything this FAQ does not cover, the teaching team decides what the deduction should be and writes the reason with it.

This is why the FAQ is worth reading before you ask or act: once an answer is here, not knowing it is not a reason.

23. What happens if I break a rule?

Talk to the teaching team first — most things are settled by explaining what happened. Where a rule has clearly been broken, the teaching team decides what follows: it can be an SP deduction, and for malpractice in a marked piece of work it also affects the marks for that work, as set out under Academic integrity. If you think a decision about your points or marks is wrong, write to the teaching team with your reasons.

The full list of penalties, and when a matter goes beyond the teaching team, will be added here.

24. Where do I see my SP?

On the case-study site (question 11): open Spurti from your home page. The SP Bank tab is your statement — every line, credit and debit, with the reason and the date — and the tabs next to it show your ViBe, Spandan, case-study and project progress against the class average. The last tab shows your tentative marks. Everything there is yours alone; you cannot see anyone else's.
